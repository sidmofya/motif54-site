Approach

Show the evidence. Make the judgment accountable.

A useful answer makes its dependencies visible.

We distinguish what a source reports from what a model calculates, what we assume and what remains unknown. AI can accelerate parts of the investigation. It does not remove the need to check evidence or take responsibility for a conclusion.

How we work

Five commitments.

01

Frame the decision and its system boundary.

Start with the decision, its timing and what sits inside and outside the question. A boundary drawn too wide hides the constraint; one drawn too narrow misses it.

02

Separate observations, calculations, estimates and assumptions.

Each statement keeps its status. A reported figure, a calculated result, an estimate and an assumption stay apart, so a reader can see what a conclusion rests on.

03

Use AI to assist retrieval, synthesis and scenario exploration.

AI helps find and organise sources, compare documents and explore how a result changes with its inputs. It does not decide what is true.

04

Challenge conclusions against primary evidence and domain knowledge.

Conclusions are checked against primary sources and industrial experience, and against what would have to be true for them to be wrong.

05

Explain implications and the uncertainty that remains.

The advice states its implications, what is still unknown and the evidence that would change it. A person is accountable for it.
